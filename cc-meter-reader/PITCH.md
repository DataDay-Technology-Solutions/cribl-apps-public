## The five-minute demo (projector or laptop; the phone is the remote)

## Likely questions and the honest answers
- **"Isn't this just Cribl Insights?"** "Insights draws this map in bytes, compares windows, and alerts on volume. It doesn't know what a byte costs at any destination, it doesn't ask what would have happened without Cribl, it doesn't put a commit and a username on a savings drop, and its notifications are alerts, not a dollar receipt for people who never log in. Those four are the app. And Meter Reader delivers through the same notification targets Insights uses, so it fits what you've already set up."
- **"Isn't this just the Monitoring page?"** "Monitoring shows bytes. This prices them at the destination, tracks the savings against your commits, and notifies. Same answer as Insights, one layer earlier."
- **"How do you know what we would have paid?"** "You tell it: per destination, what it charges per GB, and whether that data would have gone there, somewhere cheaper, or nowhere. The default assumes the same destination at full volume. Change one dropdown and the math changes with it, and the math is on screen. 'Paid' is the destination's outgoing bytes before compression, so compression never counts as savings."
- **"Cribl's route byte counters are estimates. How accurate is this?"** "We measured that: route counters read 24 to 68 percent low on reshaped events. So every minute is reconciled to Cribl's exact source and destination counters, which match delivered bytes within a fifth of a percent. Totals per source and per destination are exact; Show the math says where a split rests on an estimate."
- **"Are those real packs?"** "The functions of current Dispensary packs — the Windows XML one and Palo Alto — imported as demo pipelines, with versions and attribution in the README. The reduction you saw is measured on this data, not the README's number."
- **"Those numbers are small."** "It's a demo feed, so I show the percent, not its dollars. At 10 TB a day of firewall logs, one pack projects to about $2.5M\* a year: 10,000 GB/day × $2.25/GB\* × 30% × 365 = $2,463,750\*, where 30% is the top of the 15–30% Cribl publishes for the Palo Alto Networks pack. Projected at Cribl's published 30%; we measured 34% live on our demo feed. At 1 TB a day it's about $246K\*. Story mode runs the same engine on the built-in sample enterprise workspace, and its Receipt reads $8.1M\* a year (sample). The math is identical at your volume: put your contract rate on the Prices page and the annualized line is your number."
- **"What about Cribl's own cost?"** "It's on. This org is priced at Cribl's published list price per gigabyte ingested, no discount and no free tier, and the Receipt shows the net after Cribl and how many times Cribl paid for itself; the built-in sample enterprise workspace reads 2.8× (sample). At your volume and your contract rate the multiple is usually higher, because the SIEM price is fixed per gigabyte and the pack savings compound." (Settings → Cribl cost; the Receipt shows net and payback on MTD and Annualized.)
- **"Does it change my config?"** "Not your pipelines, routes, sources or destinations. The package you install declares reads, a pipeline preview for the what-if dry run that saves nothing, and posts that only create: an alert message in the bell, and a forwarded alert to a notification target. You see every one on the Review App screen before you confirm, and the README explains each. It creates two small Search objects — a saved search that never runs and one notification — only when you click Connect on a target, and it tells you exactly what before it does. The levers you just watched live in a separate demo build, act only on demo-tagged objects, and sit behind a confirmation."
- **"Does it keep metering when nobody has it open?"** "The package you install meters every minute while it's open, and when you reopen it, it catches up from Cribl's metrics history, so the numbers stay complete. That's what installs on every plan. If you want it metering with no tab open, run the small runner on a machine you own with an API credential — it's the same code, and it's what this org runs — or install the Enterprise variant, which runs it on a schedule."
- **"Two minutes — is that real?"** "Measured on this org across nine live runs: between 1:27 and 2:13 from the deploy to the alert, median 1:43, in demo mode with a one-minute confirmation window. The default is three minutes, and every threshold is a setting."
- **"Diurnal traffic, false positives?"** "An exponentially weighted baseline with a 24-hour memory, plus its variance, per object; a dollars-a-day floor so a big percentage drop on a flow worth cents never pages anyone; seven-day hour-of-week baselines are on the roadmap, and every threshold is a setting."
- **"Why not post to Slack directly?"** "On Standard plans an App can't declare an outbound host. Cribl's own notification targets are the better path anyway: your administrator already has them, and the Slack URL or the PagerDuty key stays in Cribl. The runner and the Enterprise variant can also post a direct webhook."
- **"Where's my Slack webhook URL stored?"** "With a Cribl notification target, it isn't stored in the app at all: it stays in Cribl's target, and the app keeps the target's id. No build stores a webhook URL: the App has no field for one. The only direct webhooks are the self-hosted runner's, and their URLs live in its own git-ignored `.env`, never in KV. No token, password, key or webhook URL is ever stored in the clear."
- **"Which Cribl APIs?"** "Inputs, outputs, routes, pipelines and the version API for commits, all reads; the metrics query the Monitoring page uses, which is in Cribl's documented API; the pipeline preview for the what-if dry run; and the notification bell, notification targets and Search notifications for delivery. Plus the App's KV store. The README lists every endpoint with its method and why."
- **"We're on workload pricing, SVCs, not GB. Does this still apply?"** "Yes, with one honest caveat. Meter Reader prices bytes, because bytes are what it can measure exactly. On workload pricing, cutting ingest lowers the indexing share of your SVCs in proportion, and searches over shaped data get cheaper, but your SVC block is fixed until renewal, and search workload is something no Cribl App can see. So on this release, enter the effective per-GB figure you use internally and read the receipt as capacity you freed. And be straight about one more thing: Splunk's own experiments show the SVC reading may not move when ingest drops; what you free is capacity, and the money shows up at your next sizing or renewal. An SVC price basis, a derived dollars-per-GB from your price per SVC and the GB per day per SVC at your workload class, plus the storage line, shown with its confidence, is designed and on the roadmap; I kept it out of this release rather than change a measured product days before the deadline."
- **"Where does this run in production? Your laptop?"** "Three places, same code. The package you install meters while the App is open and catches up from Cribl's metrics history when you reopen it; that is what judges' workspaces run. This org is metered around the clock by the runner, a small Node process on an always-on machine of mine with an org API credential, supervised and health-checked, and the footer names the host. On an Enterprise plan the same sweep runs as a scheduled backend function inside Cribl, so nothing of yours leaves Cribl."
- **"Multiple worker groups? Edge?"** "Worker groups yes; Edge fleets are next."
- **"Can I use it today?"** "Install the package from the repo and set a price. Alerts land in the Cribl bell with no setup; pick a notification target for Slack or PagerDuty. Marketplace submission is the next step."
- **"Who built it?"** "Me, solo, with Claude Code as the coding assistant. It's disclosed in the README, per the rules."

## Story mode — when you're not talking
The 90-second self-narrating loop: hook → how it works → the Meter → a change ships → watching → the alert with a name on it → Slack → Restore → the receipt → the ask. Same beats and captions as the video, so nothing you show ever disagrees with what people saw online. Use it: on the laptop at a booth or a walk-around (leave it looping with the QR on screen); on the phone at dinner; as the opener on stage while the room settles if the format is a projector; and as the fallback if the org is unreachable and Replay isn't enough. ## Never say
- "Nothing in the product does this." Insights draws the map and pushes alerts; say what it doesn't do.
- "Under a minute." It's about two minutes; the clock on the card is the number.
- Any dollars or gigabytes a day from the demo org, even as "measured live". Say the percent: "34% measured live on our demo feed." The only dollars are the projection (about $2.5M\* a year at 10 TB/day, with its math) and its ladder. The labelled sample reads $8.1M\* a year.
- "Since Friday night." Read the collecting-since caption; the org was created Saturday at the earliest.
- "Undocumented" about the metrics query. It is in Cribl's published API reference; call it "the metrics query the Monitoring page uses".
- That the package you install watches around the clock. It meters while it's open and catches up when reopened; with no tab open, it's the runner you host or the Enterprise variant that keeps metering.
- "It's in Slack" unless a Slack target is connected on that org. On the demo org it's the bell and the demo webhook receiver.
- "Read-only" about the release on its own, or "never touches config". It reads, and it posts alerts through Cribl; it never changes pipeline, route, source or destination configuration.
- "Every Monday" on its own. The runner and the Enterprise variant send the receipt on schedule; the release sends it the first time the App meters after Monday 12:00 UTC, any day that week (marked late after the first 24 hours).
- "Installed the packs." The demo imports their functions as demo pipelines.
- "Twenty-four-hour EWMA" as if it models the day. It's a 24-hour *memory*; hour-of-week is the roadmap item.
- "Vote for me" anywhere outside the conference.
